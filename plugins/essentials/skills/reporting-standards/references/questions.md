# The eight sections: what to ask, and the choices to offer

Ask by offering choices and reactions. People can't describe how their team works from a blank page, but they
recognise it at once in a list. One section per message. Every section can be skipped; a skipped one becomes an open
question in the file.

## 1. Your team
- Function: Finance / FP&A · Marketing / growth · Customer success · Operations · People / HR · Sales / revenue ops · Other
- Tools (pick any): Excel · Google Sheets · SQL · Power BI · Tableau · Looker · Python · Claude or ChatGPT · anything else
- Team name (optional)

## 2. Who reads the work (up to four readers)
For each: **who** (the team's own lead · an executive (CEO, CFO, COO) · the board or investors · a client · peers on
other teams · other), **what they decide with it** (their words), **time they give a page** (30 seconds · 2 minutes ·
10 minutes or more), **what they want first** (the answer or recommendation · the number against target · the trend ·
the detail behind it).

## 3. Where the numbers live
For each subject they report on (revenue · customers · costs · marketing spend · orders or transactions · headcount):
the **one system** it should be read from, and how often it refreshes (live · daily · weekly · at month-end close).
Push gently for one system per subject; "it depends" is worth writing down as its own rule.

## 4. Your words
For each term, offer the common definitions and "ours is different":
- **Revenue:** gross, before returns and discounts · net of returns and discounts · recognised revenue (the
  accounting figure) · cash collected
- **Active customer:** bought in the last 30 days · last 90 days · last 12 months · has a live subscription or contract
- **Churn:** cancelled a subscription or contract · did not renew at term · no purchase in a set number of days
- **Conversion rate:** orders divided by sessions · leads divided by visits · won deals divided by opportunities
- **Margin:** gross margin (revenue less cost of goods) · contribution margin (after marketing and fulfilment) · net margin
Then: "Any other word your team uses that an outsider would get wrong?"

## 5. Your best work
"Think of the piece your reader liked most. Which of these are true of it?" The answer is in the first line · it fits
on one page · numbers are rounded (thousands or millions) · every number sits beside its target · every number sits
beside last period · tables, not charts · one chart per point · plain words, no jargon · it ends with a recommendation
· caveats come at the end · caveats come first.

"And the one that went badly: what was wrong?" Too long · a number didn't match another report · a definition wasn't
clear · it answered the wrong question · too much detail for the reader · it arrived late.

"Which pieces of work should sit beside this file as examples?" (names only; they keep the files.)

## 6. Never
Pie charts · 3D or decorative charts · a metric shown without its target or comparison · more than one decimal place
· customer or client names in the output · a source number changed without saying so · a number with no source named
· anything else.

## 7. What you check by hand before anything goes out
Totals tie to the source system · counts compared with last period · dates fall in the right period · no duplicate
rows or ids · definitions match the dashboard · someone else reads it before it goes out · anything else.

## 8. Where you start (the baseline)
The two or three recurring tasks that eat the week. For each: how often (weekly · monthly · quarterly · ad hoc),
hours each time, how often it's redone after review (rarely · sometimes · often), and how much they'd trust AI with it
today, 1 to 5. Say why you ask: this is how they'll see the time they get back.
